## Greetings and politeness

"<span lang="zh">不好意思</span>" (bù hǎo yìsi) is the most useful phrase in China. Use it for "excuse me", "sorry" and to get attention politely.

### Phrases

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="zh">不好意思</span> | Bù hǎo yìsi | Sorry / Excuse me |
| <span lang="zh">你好</span> | Nǐ hǎo | Hello |
| <span lang="zh">谢谢</span> | Xièxie | Thank you |
| <span lang="zh">不客气</span> | Bú kèqi | You're welcome |
| <span lang="zh">再见</span> | Zàijiàn | Goodbye |

| <span lang="zh">你能帮我吗？</span> | Nǐ néng bāng wǒ ma? | Can you help me? |
| <span lang="zh">没事</span> | Méi shì | No problem |

## Table manners

### Chopsticks

Don't:

- Stand chopsticks upright in rice. It looks like funeral incense.
- Point at people with chopsticks.
- Tap your bowl with chopsticks. People connect it with begging.

Do:

- Put your chopsticks flat across your bowl when you do not use them.
- Share dishes family-style: serve yourself from the centre.
- When someone fills your tea, tap two fingers on the table to say thank you.

## Do's and don'ts

### Do

- Take business cards and gifts with both hands.
- Take off your shoes when you enter a home.
- Try a few words of Chinese.
- Be patient with the language barrier.
- Walk clockwise around Buddhist sites.

### Don't

- Tip in restaurants, taxis or hotels.
- Criticise or embarrass someone in public.
- Give clocks, sharp objects or sets of 4 as gifts.
- Write names in red ink.
- Touch Buddha statues for photos.

Lucky numbers: 8 (sounds like "wealth") and 6 (sounds like "smooth"). Avoid 4: it sounds like "death", and many buildings have no 4th floor.

Customs change from region to region.
