> Save these numbers before you travel. Most emergency operators speak little English.

## Emergency numbers

| Service | Number | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Police | 110 | General emergencies, theft, accidents |
| Fire | 119 | Fire |
| Ambulance | 120 | Medical emergencies |
| Traffic accidents | 122 | Road accidents |

## When you call

- Operators answer in Mandarin. Stay calm and speak slowly.
- In large cities, 110 and 120 operators may speak basic English or transfer you.
- Ask hotel staff or a local to help you make the call.
- Emergency lines do not take SMS.
- Be ready to give your location: open a map app and read the address.

## Medical emergencies

### Paying at a hospital

- Most public hospitals ask for payment before treatment (cash or card).
- International clinics take insurance but cost much more.
- Keep a photo of your insurance card and policy number on your phone.
- Know the 24-hour assistance number of your insurer.

## Your hotel can help

- Most hotels have a 24-hour front desk that can help translate an emergency call.
- Better hotels have a doctor on site or can call one.
- Hotel staff can help you talk to the police or the ambulance crew.
- Always carry your hotel's business card in Chinese. Show it to taxi drivers or responders.

## Share your location

When you cannot describe where you are in Chinese:

- Share your live location in WeChat or Amap (<span lang="zh">高德地图</span>).
- Keep a screenshot of your hotel address in Chinese for taxi drivers or responders.
- Show your GPS position in your phone's map app.

## Safety tips

- Keep copies of your passport and visa (photos on your phone).
- Register with your embassy's travel program.
- Save emergency contacts on your phone before you travel.
- Get travel insurance that covers medical evacuation.
- Know your blood type and any allergies.
- Always carry your hotel's business card in Chinese.

When in doubt, contact your embassy.
